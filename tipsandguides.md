# A collection of facts for AI prompts and how to get a usefull and nearly accurate outcomes

### Split this into sub-agents.
- Think of it like hiring several helpers instead of one. If you need the agent to search through a bunch of files, review multiple things, or research several angles, it can send out "helpers" to do all of it at once instead of one at a time. Don't use this when one task depends on finishing the task before it — like building step 2 of something before step 1 is done. That just creates confusion, not speed.

### Write a plan first Before building anything.

- Ask the Agent to lay out exactly what it's about to do — including what it's assuming. This one extra step catches wrong assumptions upfront, instead of you discovering 40 minutes in that it misunderstood what you wanted.

### 3. Interview me first Instead of guessing what you want,

- Let the agent ask you questions one at a time until it really understands the goal. The trap: the agent sometimes throws 5-10 questions at you in one message, and people only answer 2 or 3. Push it to ask one question at a time — that part really matters.

### 4. Double-check your work before showing it to me

- Tell the agent it must prove things work, not just claim they do. Without this, the agent's default move is to say "looks good, should work fine" — even when it hasn't actually confirmed anything. Asking for proof forces real verification.

### 5. "Turn what we just did into a reusable skill"

Once you and the agent have solved a problem together, ask it to package the process so you can reuse it next time. Timing matters: do this the second time you hit a similar problem, not the first — the first time through, you don't yet know which steps actually mattered.
